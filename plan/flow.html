Part 2 · runs on a cron, no clicks needed

The agent loop

Each lead carries a stage and a nextActionAt. A Convex cron wakes up, picks whatever is due, and moves it one stage forward. Autopilot sends on its own; Review stops before every send and waits for one click.

How a reply is handled

Question or objectionDraft an answer from the company profile, send it (or queue for review), stay in the thread.
InterestedStage → interested. Reply with the booking link and two concrete time suggestions.
Meeting acceptedWrite a bookings row, stage → meeting booked, stop the sequence.
Not now / out of officePause follow-ups and set a later nextActionAt.
Not interestedStage → closed lost. No more email.
UnsubscribeAdd to suppressions. Checked before every future send.

Guard rails

Rules the calls follow

  • No mock data. Every row, count and chip comes from a real call or a real record. Empty screens say what to do next.
  • 200 credits, one plan, no upgrade. Credits are our own unit: finding a page of leads 5, researching a lead 2, getting an email 20, writing or answering an email 1. First-run onboarding is free. No balance, no call.
  • A hidden hard cap behind the credits. Whatever the balance says, one trial workspace can never cost more than 100 real Enrich credits, 400 GPT calls or 80 scraped pages. Each call reserves its worst case first and settles to what the provider actually charged.
  • Our keys cannot be drained. A platform-wide daily budget sits behind every workspace limit, plus per-user rate limits, one trial workspace per verified email, a cap on total trials and a kill switch. Paid work runs only in internal actions that rebuild the request server-side.
  • White-label. This page names providers because it is an engineering map. The product never does: users see lead search, email finder, website analysis and credits. Provider names, ids and raw error text never reach the browser.
  • Free first. Enrich filter options, counts and the first 75 rows of a search cost nothing. Credits are spent only on revealing an email, and only for leads scored 2 or 3.
  • Never phone. Reveal always passes fields: ["email"]; a phone reveal costs about fifty times more.
  • Exact values only. Enrich filters are case-sensitive and a typo silently returns nothing, so GPT picks from the cached allowed values and every value is re-checked before the call.
  • One send, once. Every email goes out with an idempotency key through the send ledger, after the suppression, sending-window and daily-limit checks.
  • No model key to manage. GPT calls go through Convex AI Gateway from inside actions. The only key a user ever brings is AgentMail.
